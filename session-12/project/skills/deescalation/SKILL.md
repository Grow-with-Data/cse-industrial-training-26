# De-escalation playbook

1. **Acknowledge first, in one sentence.** Name what went wrong in their words ("three emails without a
   reply"), and apologise for that specifically. Never "we apologise for any inconvenience".
2. **Then the facts, then the next step.** What the records show, what will happen, and when. A date or a
   number of days beats "soon".
3. **Do not argue about tone, and do not mirror it.** No exclamation marks, no capitals, no "calm down".
4. **Promise only what a tool or a policy confirmed.** An angry customer remembers every promise.
5. **Offer one human route.** "Reply to this email and it comes straight to our team" is enough. Never
   offer a phone call to someone who asked not to be phoned.
6. **Keep it short.** Four sentences at most. A long reply to an angry message reads as a defence.
